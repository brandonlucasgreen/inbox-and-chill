## What it is (and isn't)

This is the most important block on the page. Lead with it.

> **It's a triage queue, not another client.**
>
> Inbox & Chill doesn't try to be a Slack app or a Linear app. You can't compose
> a message in it or edit an issue. Items flow in, you act on them — open, done,
> snooze, pin, archive — and the queue goes to zero. The moment something needs
> real work, it hands you off to the app that owns it.
>
> That's the whole idea. It's a place to find out whether anything needs you,
> which is a different job from doing the work.

---

## Why I built it

> I was opening six apps to find out whether anything needed me. Usually nothing
> did — GitHub alone was sending me 1,199 notifications, and 99% of them were
> repositories I'd once starred. The checking was the cost, not the work.
>
> So this is the app I wanted: one list, keyboard-first, that tells me the truth
> and then gets out of the way. When the list is empty, the icon in my menu bar
> is empty too.

## Features

Keep these to a sentence each. No bullets-within-bullets.

**Sources**
> Linear, GitHub, Slack, ntfy, Campsite, any JSON feed, and your terminal —
> including Claude Code, which can push you a notification when it finishes or
> needs an answer.

**Triage verbs**
> Open, done, snooze, pin, archive. Done items go to a searchable 90-day archive
> and then purge themselves. Pinned items survive everything until you unpin them.

**Real snooze, where it exists**
> Snoozing a Linear item snoozes it in Linear, not just here. Everywhere else it
> hides locally.

**A badge that tells the truth**
> The menu bar can show a total count, a high-signal-only count, a plain dot, or
> nothing. Every source decides for itself what counts as high signal, and every
> source can opt out of the badge entirely.

**Keyboard-first**
> `↑↓` to move, `⏎` to open, `⌘⏎` to open and clear, `E` for done, `S` to
> snooze, `⌘Z` to undo. Type anything to filter the list.
